## ✅ Where Devin is RIGHT

**1. There is no documented API to originate, answer, or drop a call.**

```
POST /ussd                          → start/drive a USSD session
POST /s/phone {action: sms_send}    → send an SMS
```

There is **no** `call_send`, `call_answer`, `call_drop`, or `call_transfer` action anywhere in the API surface. So the "drop scammer → callback from 100" pattern we discussed earlier **cannot be built as an API call.** I was wrong to architect around it. Devin caught that, and he is right to.

## ❌ Where Devin is WRONG (he missed the best part)

Devin says *"there's no call feature"* and *"where would it live in the UI? Nowhere."* **This is incorrect.** There IS a first-class, documented call-interception mechanism, and it is *better* than what we were planning:

### The `call.connect` plug-in point (§7)

| Step | Who asks | When | You can answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`call.connect`** | A network | **A call is about to ring one of this network's subscribers** | **allow, warn, reject** |

And the payload it hands you is *exactly* the fraud signal we need:

```json
{
  "from": "0209876543",
  "fromNetwork": "telecel",
  "callerSimRegisteredAt": "2026-11-05T16:31:00.000Z",
  "to": "0241234567",
  "toNetwork": "mtn",
  "callsBetweenThemBefore": 2,
  "numbersTheCallerHasRungToday": 3
}
```

That last field — `numbersTheCallerHasRungToday` — is the **GH19 nuisance-swarm / fan-out signal** baked into the API. You don't have to compute it; the sandbox gives it to you.

And the docs explicitly state where `warn` lands:

> *"**warn** is shown where the customer is looking at a screen: before the phone menu opens, on a payment from the phone menu, as a label on a text, and **on the incoming call screen**."*

**So the mid-call warning IS real, IS documented, and IS the intended mechanism.** You return `{decision:"warn", message:"…"}` at `call.connect`, and the victim's incoming-call screen shows the banner *before they answer*. That is prevention, not reaction — a stronger story than "drop + callback."

### The fraud simulator generates the calls for you (§13)

You never need to originate a call, because the **Fraud API plays call-based incidents live**:

> *"The fraud simulator plays fraud incidents… the fraudster gets a line, reaches the victim, the money leaves, moves on and is withdrawn. At each step your service is asked."*

The relevant kinds:
- `pin_phishing` — *"A caller posing as customer service gets the PIN and empties the wallet"* (voice-driven)
- `reversal_scam` — *"A fake 'payment received' text, then a call asking for the money to be sent back"* (text→voice→USSD, i.e. GH01)

So the correct architecture is: **the simulator rings the victim → the sandbox calls your API at `call.connect` → you score and answer `allow`/`warn`/`reject` → the sandbox acts.** That is literally Route 1 ("Your API inside an institution's flow") from the screenshots you sent me. Devin is right that *we* don't originate; he's wrong that there's "no call feature."

## ⚠️ The one honest correction on audio (for the blind/illiterate)

We must be truthful here, because a judge will probe it. **You cannot inject audio into a live call via any documented endpoint.** `warn` is a *visual* banner on the incoming-call screen. A blind or illiterate user cannot read it.

So the inclusive design is **tiered by decision, not by audio injection:**

| Risk | Your answer at `call.connect` | What the victim experiences | Who it protects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HIGH** | `reject` | The scam call **never rings**. Pretext never lands. | **Everyone** — blind, illiterate, feature-phone, smartphone. The call is blocked at the switch. |
| **MEDIUM** | `warn` | Visual banner on incoming-call screen + they choose continue/cancel | Sighted, literate users |
| **LOW** | `allow` | Normal | No friction |

For the **audio** part of your demo, the honest place is the **handset simulator page** you already have (`/handset` with `playEnglishTwiWarning`) — that proves the *voice-first intervention concept* for the vulnerable, and you pair it with the `reject`-at-`call.connect` story for the live sandbox. **Do not claim the sandbox plays your Twi audio into a real call.** Pitch it as: *"HIGH-risk calls are rejected at the network, so the pretext never reaches a user who cannot read a warning; for medium risk we show the banner, and our voice-first handset layer delivers the same warning as audio for the blind and illiterate."* That is accurate and still scores the 20 pts.
